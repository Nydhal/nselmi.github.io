Research findings

Four states (background and three face labels), one ordered window of seven cells in the preceding row, and the same deterministic rule table everywhere. Alternating triangle orientation renders the resulting rows. Seed, rule table, width, boundary, phase and palette together specify a displayed construction.

One rule family, several forms

A shared table of 120 transitions produces four small letter-like solid forms. One 81-cell seed places all four together, and the result returns to background. This verifies that particular combination and spacing; other combinations can introduce conflicting or undefined neighborhoods. Explore Letterforms · DISC-014 evidence.

Seven inputs are necessary for one recorded target

For the 308-column, 148-row Sierpiński lattice target in its recorded direction, five centered inputs are insufficient: the same input requires two different outputs. Seven inputs admit a consistent table. This is an exact finite-target result, not a claim about every orientation or every construction. Open construction · DISC-006 evidence.

The 78 Selmi 2009 transitions appear early

In the padded isolated-seed experiment, 74 transitions have appeared by generation 10, and all 78 by generation 14. None of those 78 assignments can be changed while preserving this exact row trace. This does not establish uniqueness across different seeds or encodings. Open Selmi 2009 · DISC-010 evidence.

Drawing a shape and generating it are different questions

A finite state-labelled drawing admits a rule table exactly when repeated seven-cell inputs demand the same output, with background preserved. The drawing tester checks this condition and highlights contradictions. A successful test certifies the displayed rows; spatial coherence, impossibility and infinite self-similarity require separate arguments.
